How this plan works

The golden rules

  1. Easy means easy. Most runs should be conversational, about 3–4 out of 10 effort. Your lungs will come back faster than your tendons and bones, and that gap is where injuries happen.
  2. Time, not distance. Runs are measured in minutes so you don't chase pace.
  3. Repeat, don't skip. If a week felt hard, repeat it. There is no spare week built in before the race, so if you repeat one, use the walk-run race strategy.
  4. Pain rules. General soreness is fine. Sharp, localized pain (shin, knee, Achilles, foot) that worsens while running means 2–3 days off running. If it persists, see a physio.
  5. Strength supports running. You lifted until 3 months ago, so strength returns fast. Still, no leg PRs until December. If your legs feel heavy on run days, cut a set from Monday, not a run.

Set the treadmill incline to 1%. Start at 7.5 km/h in Week 1 and raise it by 0.3–0.5 km/h only when it feels easy. Don't increase speed and duration in the same week. Use the Garmin Treadmill activity indoors and accept the calibration prompt afterward. Do Saturday long runs outdoors whenever possible.

Every run = 5 min brisk walk + the dynamic warm-up (~8 min) → the main set → 5 min walk + calf & hip-flexor stretch. Warm-up and cool-down are not counted in the run times.

Pre-run warm-up (~8 min)

Do this after a 5-min brisk walk, before every run. Keep the stretches moving: hold each rep 1–2 seconds, not 30. Long static holds before running can leave legs feeling flat. Save those for the cool-down.

#DrillRepsCue
1Upper body stretch10 arm circles each way + 10 arm swings across chestLoosens the shoulders so your arm swing stays relaxed
2Hamstring sweeps8 per legHeel forward, toes up, sweep both hands down to the floor, then step through
3Knee hugs8 per legPull the knee to your chest, rise onto the toes of the standing leg
4Quad stretch (walking)8 per legHeel to glute, 1–2 s hold, knees together, stand tall
5Piriformis stretch (walking figure-4)6 per legCradle the ankle across the opposite knee, sit back slightly
6Open the gate8 per legKnee up and out to the side in a circle. Then do 8 "close the gate" the opposite way
7World's greatest stretch4 per sideLunge, elbow toward instep, rotate and reach to the sky. Slow and controlled
8A-skips2 × 15–20 mKnee up, quick snap down under the hips, light and springy

Weeks 0–2: do A-marches (same motion, walking) instead of A-skips while your calves and Achilles adapt. Switch to skips from Week 3.

Order: upper body → gentle → bigger ranges → A-skips last, so your legs are springy right as you start running.

🏁 Race day guide

The night before & morning of

  • Saturday: a familiar carb-rich dinner, kit laid out, early to bed.
  • Race morning: eat your tested pre-long-run breakfast 2–3 hours before the start.
  • Nothing new on race day: no new shoes, clothes, gels, or breakfast.

Pacing

SectionPlan
km 1–3Deliberately slower than feels natural. Everyone goes out too fast.
km 4–7Settle into long-run effort.
km 8–10If there's energy left, push gradually.

Walk-run backup

If continuous running hasn't clicked by Week 7, race with run 9 min / walk 1 min. Many runners finish faster this way than by running until they crack.

Expected finish time

Most people returning after a long break land around 65–75 min. Use your Week 7 5K test × 2.1 for a personal estimate. Goals: A) finish strong, B) run the second half no slower than the first, C) the time target from your 5K test.

Strength library

Finish every set with 2–3 good reps in reserve. Form beats weight. The weekly cards show your sets for that week. Full progression is below.

Mobility & core (Sunday, ~20 min)

  • Hip flexor stretch: 2 × 30 s per side
  • Pigeon pose: 2 × 30 s per side
  • Hamstring stretch: 2 × 30 s per side
  • Calf stretch on a step (straight and bent knee): 2 × 30 s each
  • Cat-cow × 10, 90/90 hip switches × 8
  • Front plank: 3 × 30–45 s
  • Foam roll calves, quads, and glutes (optional)

Setting up runs in Garmin

  1. Garmin Connect app → More → Training & Planning → Workouts → Create Workout → Running.
  2. Warm Up: Time 5:00, No Target.
  3. Delete the default Run step, then tap Add Repeat and set the repeat count (e.g., 8).
  4. Inside the repeat: Run = Time (e.g., 1:00), Recover = Time (e.g., 2:00), both No Target.
  5. Cool Down: Time 5:00. Save using the name shown on each run card (e.g., W1 Tue – R1/W2×8).
  6. Drag it onto your Garmin calendar, sync, and on the watch go to Run → Training → Workouts → Do Workout.

Quick alternative: Run activity settings → Alerts → Run/Walk. It loops endlessly and doesn't count rounds, so stop it yourself. Menu names vary slightly by watch model.

If the race is earlier than 13 Dec

6 Dec: merge Weeks 3 & 4 (use Week 4's runs), make Week 7's Saturday the peak long run at 60 min, then taper the final week as shown in Week 9.

For anything earlier, the timeline needs a rebuild.
